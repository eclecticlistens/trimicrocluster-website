Core & Advanced

One musical direction.
Two different settings.

A standalone instrument should not require someone else's studio. A personal performance system should not be held back by the need to fit every setup.

Why separate them?

TMC grew in two directions. One was a self-contained instrument for norns. The other was Nick's wider environment for composing, performing, processing and recording music with several pieces of equipment.

Those are related ambitions, but they have different practical needs. Separating them lets the project share a useful instrument without requiring every reader to reproduce a particular hardware arrangement.

Core: an instrument to share

Core is intended to offer a complete standalone musical experience, using norns without depending on the specialised Advanced setup. Stereo operation was established as the primary direction in the early development discussions; additional output arrangements were a separate concern.

The aim is to release Core freely, with the necessary credits and installation guidance. It is still being prepared. Compatibility work is particularly important for older original norns hardware, where an arrangement that runs on the shield cannot simply be assumed to perform equally well.

There is no Core download on this site yet. Its release notes will identify the version, the hardware actually tested and any known limits.

Advanced: a personal system

Advanced is where TMC meets external percussion, MIDI destinations, individual audio paths, effects and recording choices. The development conversations include the ES-8, TX-6, Bitbox Micro, Torso S-4, SSL 2+, TP-7 and Bitwig. Their roles have changed as the working setup developed.

The important question is not how much equipment can be connected. It is which part makes a musical decision, which part renders the sound and which part captures or transforms it. Nick's preference has been to keep TMC central to the generative organisation rather than hand its character to a conventional groovebox workflow.

Advanced's code will not be offered for download because it is tailored to that personal setup. Its design can still be useful to other people: the site can explain a routing decision, a percussion relationship or a listening result without presenting the whole system as a supported product.

Getting Core ready

A release needs more than code that runs once. Another person needs to install it, understand its controls and know which conditions have actually been tested. The preparation includes separating hardware-specific configuration, reviewing dependencies and credits, and listening on the intended devices.

Hardware differences belong in that account rather than in small print. The original norns family includes CM3 and CM3+ machines according to monome's documentation. TMC's final compatibility statement will follow testing of an identified build, not a general assumption about the platform.

Sources and release context

The stereo-first direction was reaffirmed on 17 August 2026. The distinction between a standalone community edition and a personal external-hardware system was explicit by 28 September. On 23 October, Nick confirmed that Core is the version to open for download and Advanced is to be documented without distributing its source.
