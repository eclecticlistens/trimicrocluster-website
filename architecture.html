The Conductor · AOS · Layer behaviour

How does TMC organise musical time?

Choosing a note is one decision. Deciding when a part joins, when it gives way and how it returns is another. TMC separates those questions.

More than tempo

Listen to an evolving piece for long enough and its shape is not explained by tempo alone. A sustained sound may remain while another part falls silent. A short figure may return against a different background. The pace of these changes can matter as much as the spacing between individual notes.

This is the sense in which TMC's Conductor is temporal: it deals with relationships across time. Its purpose is not simply to count beats. It helps organise when applicable parts can participate while leaving room for each part's own musical behaviour.

The name describes a coordinating role. It does not mean that one central process has to prescribe every pitch, articulation and sound in the instrument.

Permission is not a score

One important mechanism is the distinction between PLAY and IDLE. For a layer governed by that gate, PLAY permits participation; IDLE withholds it. The permission is a condition under which the layer operates, not a guarantee that a note will sound at every moment.

The layer still has its own musical logic. It may choose a phrase, generate an event or leave space. That distinction allows the overall activity to change without reducing all parts to copies of one sequence.

SubHarm is a deliberate exception. In the documented design it remains outside the Conductor's PLAY/IDLE gate. That does not mean it has no relationship to the rest of TMC, only that this particular permission rule is not applied to it.

Anchor / Orbit / Shimmer

TMC also needs a language for relationships within the music. Anchor, Orbit and Shimmer — AOS — provide three roles. They describe different tendencies in a shared musical world, rather than three fixed instruments.

AOS gives different parts a common vocabulary without requiring every sound source to interpret a role in exactly the same way. A sustained voice and a short resonant event have different musical possibilities even when their behaviour is described with the same role.

Roles, behaviour and AUTO are different questions

The early architecture distinguished AOS from a second vocabulary, TRI: Still, Drift, Shine and Mix. The distinction is useful to keep in mind when listening or reading a settings record.

What is its role?

AOS describes a musical relationship: Anchor, Orbit or Shimmer.

How does it behave?

TRI describes the layer's behavioural approach. Its detailed effect depends on the layer and implementation.

May it participate?

The Conductor's permission logic addresses activity over time for applicable layers.

Who makes the choice?

AUTO is a control setting for automatic selection. It is not a fourth AOS role or a synonym for Shimmer.

That is why an audio label should separate the Conductor setting from the roles of the active layers. A take labelled AUTO may still contain several different roles and changes within the same minute. A useful listening record describes the settings that were actually used, rather than treating the filename as a complete score.

The pitch world

TMC's development began with Bloom-related pitch material and cellular ideas. The project gradually acquired its own musical organisation, while a separate DPool approach served the lower SubHarm layer. The aim became to retain useful microtonal relationships without making the final instrument depend on its starting point in the same way.

That history matters because the current architecture was not designed in a single step. Removing an inherited sound, keeping a useful behaviour and rewriting the relationship between them can be separate decisions. The journal follows those changes without presenting a development intention as a completed public release.

Where the human fits

The autonomous part of TMC operates inside a musical direction chosen by its maker. Nick decides which relationships are worth exploring, listens to the resulting behaviour and changes the design when it fails to produce the intended experience.

AI assistance belongs to that development process: it helps with discussion, research, programming and documentation. It should not be confused with the Conductor running inside the instrument. The Conductor is a designed musical system, not a chat model deciding what to play.

Architecture notes and scope

This explanation draws on the August 2026 architecture discussions, the recorded AOS/TRI distinction and the subsequent October development context. It is a conceptual guide, not a function-by-function audit of the latest Lua and SuperCollider files. The exact scope of an AUTO control and any layer-specific exception should be stated in release-specific documentation.

For the underlying platform, see monome's norns documentation, which describes its Lua control scripts and SuperCollider sound engines. That platform documentation does not by itself establish TMC's compatibility or performance on a particular device.
