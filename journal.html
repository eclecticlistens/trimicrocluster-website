Development Journal

A documented history of ideas, changes, listening tests and discarded approaches. The dates below represent decisions and discussions, not public releases.

In brief

The journal preserves decisions and differences between evidence types. A code-cleanup test, a successful listening session and a hardware performance test are not interchangeable.

Documented development chronology

August 2026

The instrument takes its own identity

AOS — Anchor, Orbit and Shimmer — developed as the preferred language for musical relationships. The name TriMicroCluster connected the three-way organisation with microtonal material and interacting clusters.

Creative vocabulary
August–September 2026

From Bloom-derived ideas to a distinct TMC architecture

Early scale/pitch and cellular concepts were re-examined. Later discussions aimed to remove direct Bloom dependencies while preserving useful microtonal design ideas. That direction should not be confused with proof of a completely finished migration.

Developing architecture
September–October 2026

Core and Advanced responsibilities diverge

Standalone release requirements were separated from the multi-device recording and percussion environment. The public edition and personal performance system now have deliberately different software distribution policies.

Release scope
October 2026 — code audit

Cleanup and regressions

A read-only audit and staged cleanup removed unused helpers and legacy material. A reported 15 local tests passed, which supports specific local checks, not a full sound-engine or hardware validation.

Local tests, not whole-system proof
12 October 2026

Pulse / Fracture discontinued in favour of Objects V2

The artist explicitly chose to stop pursuing Pulse and Fracture development and focus on the eight-pad Objects ensemble with individual motifs and Conductor participation.

Accepted design direction
20 October 2026

Accepted Advanced Objects listening baseline

After correcting Pad 8 Slicer participation and Pads 1–6 relationship allocation, the artist accepted the resulting musical behaviour after a roughly twenty-minute listening session. That baseline was backed up.

Listening acceptance
21 October 2026

A proposed four-voice generative MIDI part

An additional Conductor-led four-voice part was discussed for the Advanced recording environment. It was deliberately conceived without new Grid nudges or manual controls. It was a proposed design, not a completed implementation.

Proposal only
23 October 2026

Website and accessible publication method

The archive's direction became explicit: musical architecture, development decisions, original research references and a central account of dyslexia and AI-assisted creative work.

Publishing decision

A worked example: Objects V2

Rather than programming conventional drum patterns, the artist wanted changing relationships, sparse events and independent motifs that could develop across different timescales without overwhelming the rest of TMC.

Before · Pulse / Fracture

Earlier candidate rhythm profiles. The approach was set aside as the artist committed to Objects.

After · Objects

Eight-pad system with per-pad independence; the accepted October test involved the ensemble participation model and Pad 8 Slicer.

Advanced implementation snapshot: Pad 7 and Pad 8

Pad 7 retained a short-trigger granular role, with further held-note and CC experimentation deferred. Pad 8 used eight Slicer slices. The accepted baseline included channel 10 note 43, channel 12 CC70 slice selection values 0, 16, 32, 48, 64, 96, 112 and 127, a six-event motif [1, 5, 3, 6, 5, 8], and a 1.5-host-beat onset spacing.

These values are specific to the Advanced listening baseline, not general Bitbox hardware defaults.

Different tests answer different questions

  • Automated checks: useful for specific regressions and expected program behaviour.
  • Listening: tests whether behaviour is musically useful to the artist.
  • Hardware testing: shows whether a specific norns/firmware system can sustain the sound engine and I/O requirements.

Original aluminium norns tests revealed CPU/audio difficulties with Rings Cells that were less apparent on the shield. Future compatibility statements must account for those differences rather than extrapolate from a single platform.

What will be added as source evidence

Selected dated conversation excerpts, accepted backups, implementation diffs and short listening examples will eventually accompany these entries. At present, the summaries are editorial reconstructions of documented discussions, not pretend verbatim quotes.
